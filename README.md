## About Me

I'm **Jayant Kumar**, an undergraduate cybersecurity researcher working at the intersection of **post-quantum cryptography, quantum cryptanalysis, authentication protocols, and applied security engineering**.

My current research investigates the security of lightweight, noise-based authentication schemes against quantum-enabled adversaries, with particular interest in **superposition access to protocol oracles** and the structural leakage that can emerge under such models.

Alongside theoretical cryptography, I develop open-source research software for reproducible cryptographic analysis, TLS security assessment, and post-quantum readiness measurement.

---

## Research Focus

* Quantum cryptanalysis of symmetric and lightweight authentication protocols
* Learning Parity with Noise (LPN) and deterministic LPN variants
* Spectral and Walsh–Hadamard analysis of Boolean functions
* Superposition-query security models (Q1 / Q2)
* Siftingless Quantum Key Distribution and finite-key analysis
* LDPC code design and density evolution for QKD information reconciliation
* Post-Quantum Cryptography deployment and migration
* ML-KEM and hybrid TLS
* Cryptographic Bill of Materials (CBOM)
* Empirical assessment of post-quantum readiness across TLS infrastructure

# Publications & Research

### Spectral Characterization of Superposition Leakage in Deterministic-LPN Authentication Protocols

**2026 · Preprint · Zenodo · CC BY 4.0**

**DOI:** [10.5281/zenodo.21934507](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21934507)

A study characterising structural leakage in deterministic-LPN authentication protocols under superposition queries using **Walsh–Hadamard spectral analysis**, together with an information-theoretic treatment of the keyed-secret setting.

---

### An Empirical Study of Post-Quantum Cryptographic Readiness Across Enterprise TLS Infrastructure

**2026 · Research Study · with N. Soni**

A measurement study covering **400 TLS endpoints** across banking and multi-sector datasets, investigating TLS configurations, cryptographic deployment, and indicators of post-quantum readiness.

The study is supported by the **AegisGuard** scanning and analysis pipeline.

---

### Reassessing Siftingless Quantum Key Distribution Under Matched Finite-Block and Finite-Key Conditions

**Research in Progress · Project Q-FAIR**

A research project reproducing and extending **BESC/LDPC density-evolution thresholds** for siftingless QKD under finite-block and finite-key conditions.

**Current status:** Phase 1 complete.

# Research Software

## AegisGuard

**Post-Quantum-Aware TLS Security Assessment Platform**

A modular security assessment platform designed to evaluate public-facing TLS infrastructure and identify cryptographic configurations relevant to post-quantum migration.

### Capabilities

* TLS version and cipher-suite analysis
* Multi-stage TLS security assessment
* Cryptographic Bill of Materials (CBOM) generation
* ML-KEM / hybrid PQC detection
* Certificate-chain validation
* Cryptographic risk assessment
* SQLite-based scan history
* Reproducible security assessment workflow

AegisGuard forms the measurement backbone of the **400-endpoint PQC/TLS readiness study**.

---

## AegisGuard-Q

**Quantum Cryptanalysis Research Toolkit**

A reference implementation and experimental toolkit supporting the superposition-leakage research.

### Capabilities

* Walsh–Hadamard Transform analysis
* Protocol response-function analysis
* Independent NumPy statevector cross-validation
* Regression testing
* JSON and Markdown experiment exports
* Reproducible computational experiments

**Current validation:** 25-test regression suite verified across Linux and Windows.

---

## Q-FAIR

**Finite-Key Siftingless QKD Research Codebase**

A research codebase for reproducing and extending finite-key threshold calculations for siftingless QKD.

### Current Components

* `besc.py`
* `distributions.py`
* `de.py`
* `table1.py`

### Current Work

* BESC / LDPC density-evolution pipeline
* Quantized LLR-grid box-plus check-node implementation
* Unit-test framework
* Reproducible experiment configuration
* Packaged Python research environment

**Phase 1:** Complete

**Next:** Fine-grid Gate 1 sweep → PEG construction → Sum-product decoding → Rate adaptation
